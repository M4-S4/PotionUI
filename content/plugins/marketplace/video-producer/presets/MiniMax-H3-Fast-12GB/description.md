Generate a short video and its soundtrack together from one prompt with FastVideo's **FastH3 8-Step V2**, set up for a graphics card with **12 GB of VRAM**. It is the MiniMax-H3-Fast preset with a memory profile on top: the same pipeline and the same controls, with smaller files and lighter defaults. FastH3 is a DMD2-distilled repack of MiniMax-H3 that finishes a clip in exactly 8 steps; it runs at a fixed 24 fps for up to 15 seconds and needs no guidance scale or negative prompt.

**What the 12 GB profile changes.** The model pickers recommend the int8 ConvRot FastH3 DiT, the nvfp4 text encoder, the int8 ConvRot video VAE and the fp32 audio VAE. The form starts at 832×480 for 124 frames (5.17 s at 24 fps), one clip, the fixed 8 steps with the Res multistep sampler and the Simple scheduler, and a 1× keyframe detail budget. 480×832, 960×544 and 544×960 are in the size list, and so is the 1344×768 canvas, which is far heavier on a 12 GB card.

**The text encoder lives in system RAM.** The 32B text encoder alone (14.6 GB in nvfp4) is larger than the card. This profile expects the native backend to stream it from system RAM instead of moving it onto the GPU: set the backend's text encoder placement option to stream from RAM (it ships with PotionUI's native memory profiles). Without it the text encoder runs out of GPU memory before sampling starts. Plan on **64 GB of system RAM**: the encoder, the DiT layers that do not fit on the card and the rest of the process share it. Streaming is slower than a card that holds everything; the 8-step schedule keeps the sampling short, but the encoding step and every step still take longer than on a 24 GB card.

**Same limits as MiniMax-H3-Fast.** There is no References mode and no Upscale mode. The sparse attention defaults (SLA with a dense warm-up) approximate the sparsity FastH3 was trained toward; dense attention is always a safe fallback.
